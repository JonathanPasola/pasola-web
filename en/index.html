I · Why Sumba

South-west coast, first passes — April.

An island kept
intact.

Sumba, in the Lesser Sunda archipelago, lies between the Indian Ocean to the south and the Savu Sea to the north. The south-west coast — where PASOLA takes its place — has been left untouched by the transformations that reshaped its neighbors: a coastline intact, hills held in silence, a low and patient light.

The island distinguishes itself by what it has not seen. No mass tourism, no hotel rush. Its coast and its traditions have kept their own cadence: the equestrian Pasola, the stonework lineage, the woven hinggi.

Along this coast, the estate spans fifteen hectares. The current phase occupies about half of it — twenty-one freehold residences, distributed across three distinct landscape zones. The remaining land is held under an independent program, outside the scope of this phase.

III · Our Vision
PASOLA · Issue 01 · May 2026 · Sumba

A lifetime of building,
one conviction.

A letter from the founders · Jennifer & Jonathan

Before Sumba, fifteen years building around the world — state palaces, hotels, major projects. Time enough to learn what lasts. And what fades.

In parallel, twenty years across media and luxury brands, in seven Asian markets. Learning how a brand story holds — or doesn't — against a culture that isn't its own.

"An estate is not conceived as a product. It holds through time, it is passed on, it settles into the landscape rather than reshaping it."

Sumba was not a whim. It is a considered answer — where to build, for whom, and with what respect for the place. On the south-west coast, across fifteen hectares of contiguous land, PASOLA Signature Estate gives form to that conviction.

Pasola · The seasonal rite

The equestrian rite.

Each year, on the south-west coast, the festival from which the estate borrows its name unfolds at the meeting of harvest and rain. Riders on horseback face one another across the rice fields, wooden spears in hand — a measured rite that predates colonial maps and continues today.

The estate honours the cadence — measured, patient, in season — never the spectacle. We borrowed the name, not the rite : restraint over performance, presence over display.

Hinggi · The woven inheritance

The woven gesture.

A textile language transmitted by hand across generations. Indigo on cotton, motifs of horses and crocodiles, geometries unchanged since the 16th century. Each piece belongs to a lineage — colors and patterns carry the memory of a clan.

Each PASOLA interior carries one — never as ornament, always as transmission. The hinggi enters the residence as an inheritance, not as decoration.

Architecture · Uma — the high-pitched roof

The conical roofs.

The traditional Sumbanese house — the Uma Bokulu — rises in alang-alang grass to a sharp central peak, anchoring families across centuries. Its silhouette is the signature of the Sumbanese built landscape.

The estate stands within this inherited landscape — local craftsmen, materials from the island, economy of gestures. The heritage is honoured as cultural neighbourhood, not as a model to replicate.
